Chapter 5: Generalization

Learning Objectives

  • Explain the difference between training loss and test loss, and why the second one is the one that matters
  • Recognize overfitting on a plot: training loss still going down while test loss gets worse
  • Overfit a dataset by hand, and pay the price on new points
  • Read a flexibility plot: underfitting on one side, overfitting on the other
  • State the golden rule: test points are for measuring, never for choosing

Motivation

  • Three times now this series has said that a loss below the generating recipe's is a warning, not a win, and three times you had to take our word for it, because on the training points the lower loss really is lower.
  • This chapter measures it instead. The idea: a model's job is not to do well on the points it trained on. Its job is to do well on points it has never seen. Those are different things, and the gap between them is the whole game.
  • The plan: hold some points back, watch a too-big network win on the training points and lose on the new ones, do the same thing with your own hand, and then map out where the good models are found.

1. Held-Out Points: the Train / Test Split

  • The data comes from chapter 2's exact recipe (the same three sigmoid blocks, with the noise turned up a little), but this time we keep the parameter values on the table since the point of this chapter is elsewhere. We generated 80 points and split them:
    • 20 training points (filled gray dots): the only points the network is ever allowed to learn from;
    • 60 test points (open circles): set aside during training, used only to measure.
  • Both sets come from the same recipe with the same noise, so a model that truly learned the recipe scores about the same on both. A model that only memorized its 20 training points does not.
  • Note that 20 training points is not much, and that is on purpose: small datasets are where the danger is greatest.

2. Watch a Big Network Overfit

  • The network below has 12 blocks (37 parameters) for data that was made with 3. By chapter 3's counting, that is far more flexibility than the function needs, and with only 20 training points, more parameters than points.
  • Press train and read the two plots together:
    • Left: the fit. Filled dots are training points, open circles are test points. The purple curve is trained on the filled dots only.
    • Right: the two losses, epoch by epoch. The dark curve is the training loss, the orange curve is the test loss, and the dashed lines are what the generating recipe scores on each set.
  • Early on, both losses fall together: the network is learning the real shape, and the real shape helps on both sets. Then the curves separate: the training loss keeps going down, below its dashed line and beyond, while the test loss turns around and goes back up. From that moment on, every epoch makes the model better at its 20 points and worse at its job.
  • Look at the purple curve after training: it bends hard to pass near individual training dots, and the open circles are left behind. That wiggle is what memorizing looks like.

  • Drag the replay slider to the point where the orange curve is lowest and look at the fit: the model at that moment is the best this network ever was. Every epoch after that point makes it worse. (Stopping there on purpose is called early stopping, one of the standard ways to deal with overfitting; more on those in a later chapter.)

3. Your Turn to Overfit

  • In chapter 2 you could beat the network by drawing a curve through every dot. Time to collect on that. Press and drag across the plot below to draw a curve through the 20 training dots. Try to pass through every single one: your training loss can go far below anything the generating recipe scores.

  • For comparison, we also trained chapter 2's plain 3-block network on the same 20 training points. It cannot pass through every dot, it does not have the flexibility, so you will beat it easily on the training loss. Watch what happens on the test points.
  • This is the whole trade in one picture: every wiggle you put in to catch a training dot is a guess that the wiggle is real. The noise made sure most of those guesses are wrong.

4. The Flexibility Plot

  • So far: too much flexibility memorizes the points (this chapter), too little cannot follow the function at all (chapter 3). Let's map the whole range. The button below trains 10 networks, with 1 to 10 blocks, all on the same 20 training points, and plots both losses against the number of blocks.
  • Reading the finished plot:
    • Left side (1 block): both losses high. The model cannot represent the function; more training will not help. Chapter 3 called this underfitting.
    • Right side (many blocks): training loss low and still going down, test loss high and rising. The extra blocks are spent memorizing noise.
    • The bottom of the test curve sits at 2 to 3 blocks, the scale of the true recipe. With only 20 noisy points, even the true recipe's flexibility is almost more than the data can support.
  • The test curve is not perfectly smooth from one block count to the next: each network is a separate training run, and gradient descent has its own luck. Read the trend, not the individual bumps.
  • The training loss alone would tell you to keep adding blocks forever. This plot is why nobody chooses a model by its training loss.

5. The Golden Rule

  • The test set is for measuring, never for choosing. The moment you use it to pick the number of blocks, or the stopping epoch, or anything else, you have, in effect, trained on it, and it is no longer a fair measure of performance on new data.
  • A note about this page: we showed the test loss during training, which is exactly what the rule says not to do. That is fine for teaching (the gradient never saw a test point, and we are not choosing anything), but in real work the choosing is done on a third split, the validation set, and the test set is used once, at the very end.
  • The rule is easy to state and easy to break without noticing. It gets its own treatment when we tune real models.

Chapter Highlights

  1. Training loss measures the points the model saw; test loss measures the job. They can move in opposite directions, and when they do, the model is overfitting.
  2. A too-flexible network on too few points wins on training data by memorizing noise, and is punished for every memorized wiggle on new data. You did it by hand, so you know exactly how it feels.
  3. Flexibility is a trade: too little gives underfitting (chapter 3), too much gives overfitting (this chapter), and the best test loss sits near the scale of the true function.
  4. The low point of the test curve is a moment worth stopping at: early stopping is one of several ways to deal with overfitting that we will meet.
  5. The golden rule: test points measure, they never choose. Choosing belongs to a validation set.
